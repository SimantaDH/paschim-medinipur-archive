Licensing & Ethics

CC BY-NC 4.0 for this project's scholarship, with explicit safeguards for community-held material.

The Short Version

Creative Commons Attribution–NonCommercial 4.0 (CC BY-NC 4.0)

The scholarly apparatus of this project — the site's text, structure, design, bibliography and analysis — is shared under CC BY-NC 4.0. Field recordings, interview transcripts and performed songs remain the property of the individual participants and communities of Rahatpur, Kanasol, Kheerpai and Naya, used here with consent for research and educational purposes. The four elements of that licence, as they apply here:

Attribution — required

Reuse of this project's own text, analysis or design must credit Simanta Nandi and Disha Pramanik, "From Oral Tradition to Digital Archive" (2026), with a link back to this site where practical.

ShareAlike — not required

You are not obliged to license a derivative work under the same terms, unlike a BY-NC-SA licence. We'd appreciate it if you did, but it isn't a condition here.

Commercial use — not permitted

No part of this project's text, design, images, audio or transcripts may be used for commercial purposes without prior written permission. For any commercial enquiry, contact nandisimanta534@gmail.com or pramanikd389@gmail.com.

Adaptation — permitted

Remixing, translating or building on this material for non-commercial, attributed purposes is welcome — including classroom use, further research, or community-led extensions of the archive itself.

CC BY-NC 4.0 and Indigenous Data Sovereignty — Understanding the Tension

An open licence is not automatically an ethical one

A standard open licence like CC BY-NC assumes a single rights-holder freely choosing to share. Community cultural material doesn't always fit that model cleanly — a recording of a sacred rite, a family's own song, a priest's description of ritual practice carries interests beyond the two researchers who recorded it. This project tries to hold both commitments honestly, rather than quietly picking one and calling it settled:

A non-public layer for sacred material

Entries identified as sacred or restricted during fieldwork are held in a non-public layer pending direct consultation with the relevant community and practitioners, rather than published by default under the open licence above.

Contributor IP until consent is given

A participant's own words, in first person, are treated as that participant's intellectual property until they explicitly consent to open publication — not automatically folded into the project's own CC BY-NC release.

Scope of the open licence

CC BY-NC 4.0 applies squarely to this project's own scholarly apparatus — the researchers' writing, analysis, structure and design — and to community-sourced data only where specific consent for open licensing has been given.

Data Shield — a non-scraping clause

As an additional contractual layer beyond the CC licence itself, material on this site may not be scraped, bulk-downloaded or used to train machine-learning or AI systems without separate written permission — intended specifically to prevent AI-training use that a standard open licence alone does not block.

This approach draws on the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance and on platforms like Mukurtu, built for granular, culturally specific access protocols, as a baseline alongside Dublin Core and CIDOC-CRM for shared metadata.
