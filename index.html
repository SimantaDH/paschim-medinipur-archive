Two Living Traditions

Not a museum piece. Still being performed.

Gajan is danced, sung and endured on the body every spring. Patachitra is painted and sung scroll by scroll in Naya village, where every one of 176 households shares the surname Chitrakar — "artist." Both are documented here through the people who actually keep them alive.

Gajan

Five field voices — a devotee, a priest, a performer, an observer, a diaspora son — on Dandi Kata, Jhiv Phora, Charak and Agni Pariksha.

Patachitra

Natural pigments, sung scrolls, and twenty-seven sources mapping where this craft already lives online — before this archive adds one more.

Built to Last, Not Just to Launch

Three gates, nothing hidden behind a login

This project follows the University of Victoria's Endings Project principles for digital longevity — open formats, no fragile tech stack, and an honest accounting of what it can and can't promise. No fabricated AR/VR, no sampled "ritual" audio dressed up as field recordings, no copyrighted music. What you'll find instead: real interviews, a synthesized ambient soundscape built in the browser, and a narrator that reads the page aloud.
