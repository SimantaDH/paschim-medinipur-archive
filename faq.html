Governance & Contact

Frequently Asked Questions

Scope, consent, licensing, accessibility — and the limits we're upfront about.

Fifteen questions this project gets asked most — about scope, consent, licensing, accessibility, and the honest limits of what a static site can do.

What is this project?+
A digital-humanities archive documenting Gajan ritual and Patachitra scroll-painting traditions in Paschim Medinipur, West Bengal — built from original fieldwork, interviews, and research rather than secondhand or scraped material.
Who built it, and why?+
Simanta Nandi and Disha Pramanik, MA students in Digital Humanities & Social Sciences at IIT (ISM) Dhanbad. See Our Team for full bios and About the Project for the fuller brief.
What is Gajan?+
An annual Shiva-centred festival in rural Bengal combining ritual performance, fasting, bodily discipline and community devotion — documented here through five field interviews rather than from the outside looking in. See Explore → Gajan.
What is Patachitra?+
A scroll-painting and song tradition centred on Naya village, Pingla Block, where every one of 176 households carries the surname Chitrakar. Scrolls are painted with natural pigments and sung panel by panel as they unroll. See Explore → Patachitra.
Can I use material from this site?+
This project's own text, design and analysis are shared under CC BY-NC 4.0 — free to reuse non-commercially with attribution. Field recordings and participants' own words carry additional protections; see Licensing & Ethics before reusing anything sourced directly from an interview.
How was the field data collected?+
Through key-informant interviews across five roles per tradition, recorded on audio and video where participants consented, then transcribed. The full interview structure is documented in the project's methodology.
How is consent handled?+
Participants' own words are treated as their intellectual property until they consent to open publication. Sacred or restricted material is held back from public release pending community consultation, rather than published by default.
Is the AR/VR real?+
Yes — AR / VR Mode uses the browser's actual navigator.xr WebXR API (via three.js's ARButton/VRButton), with real feature detection: it offers a genuine immersive session on a WebXR-capable phone or headset, and plainly says "not supported" rather than faking one elsewhere. The two 3D scenes themselves — a Gajan ritual space and a Patachitra studio — are original procedural geometry and hand-painted canvas textures, with one credited exception (see Technical Credits on Sources & Scholarship): a small, plainly licensed rigged human figure.
Is the ambient sound real ritual audio?+
No — it's synthesized entirely in your browser using the Web Audio API (oscillators, filtered noise, and a slow bell-like pulse). No sampled, copyrighted or ritual audio was used for the ambient soundscape; the real field recordings are the actual interview clips on the Explore pages.
What accessibility features does the site have?+
Adjustable text size, a dark/light mode toggle, a built-in narrator that reads any major section aloud using your browser's own text-to-speech, and a Bengali/English translate widget in the footer of every page.
Is the site available in Bengali?+
Mostly, in two different ways. The footer's translate control (a Google Translate embed) machine-translates any page on request, so nuance in culturally specific terms (see the glossary) is best checked against the English original. The cinematic films in AR / VR Mode are different: their captions and narration are hand-translated into বাংলা, not machine-translated, and spoken aloud in Bengali when your browser has a Bengali text-to-speech voice installed.
How do I cite this project?+
Visit Cite This Project for ready-made MLA 9th, APA 7th, Chicago and BibTeX citations, with a one-click copy button for each.
How do I contact the researchers?+
Both project emails are listed on the Contact page. Use them for commercial-use requests, corrections, community consultation about sacred content, or general enquiries.
Where does the site's data and research come from?+
A combination of original fieldwork (interviews, recordings, transcripts), a twenty-seven-source literature review on Patachitra, and an independently gathered map of Patachitra's existing digital footprint. Full source list on Sources & Scholarship.
Is this a finished, final archive?+
No, and it isn't meant to be read as one. Five interviews and one craft collective cannot represent every practitioner or village; this is a documented starting point, built following the Endings Project's principle that a digital-humanities project should be honest about its own edges rather than claim to be exhaustive.
