Credits & Acknowledgements

Everything this site was built with

A full account of what went into this build and where it came from — the people, the fonts, the code, the sound, the pictures. Anything not listed here as borrowed is the work of the two of us.

People — first and foremost

The interview participants, who gave their time, their accounts and their consent: Dipak Pramanik, Manjulata Pramanik, Soumen Chakrabarty, Ankan Karmakar and Nirmal Pramanik for Gajan; the Chitrakar artists of Naya, Pingla, including Shiuli Chitrakar, Ruksana Chitrakar and Sabera Chitrakar, for Patachitra. Nothing here would exist without them, and their recorded words remain their own.

Institutional

IIT (ISM) Dhanbad, Department of Humanities & Social Sciences, where both researchers are enrolled. Earlier training that shaped the method: the Summer Research Internship Programme at IIST Thiruvananthapuram, the 15-Day UNESCO World Heritage Education Programme at Visva-Bharati, a British Library archival-digitisation workshop run with ILSR, and a research internship at IIT Kharagpur.

Photographs & images

Portrait photographs on Our Team were supplied by the researchers themselves and are used with their consent. Field photographs and recordings were made by the research team during fieldwork, with participants' permission. There are no stock photographs on this site and no images scraped from elsewhere.

Artwork & motifs

The Chitragāthā logo, the alpana dot-rings, the Patachitra-style background tile, the drifting folk-art frieze and every illustration and icon were drawn for this project as original SVG. They are made in the spirit of the tradition's visual vocabulary — fish, lotus, bird, sun-face, vine, procession — and are not reproductions of any existing scroll, painting or artist's work.

Sound

The ambient soundscape is generated live in your browser with the Web Audio API — oscillators, filtered noise and synthesized percussion over a Bhairavi-leaning scale, with a dhak-patterned eight-beat cycle. It is an original composition system written for this site. No sampled audio, no recorded music and no ritual recording is used as decoration anywhere. The genuine field recordings appear only as clearly labelled interview clips on the Explore pages.

3D scenes

The Gajan ritual space and Patachitra studio in AR / VR Mode are procedural geometry with canvas-painted textures, written for this project. One asset is borrowed and credited in full: the rigged human figure, "Rigged Figure" from the Khronos glTF-Sample-Assets collection, © 2017 Cesium, licensed CC BY 4.0, used unmodified and recoloured at runtime.

Software & libraries

three.js (MIT licence) for the WebGL scenes, WebXR session handling and glTF loading. GSAP with ScrollTrigger (standard "no charge" licence) for scroll-reveal sequencing, with a plain IntersectionObserver fallback if it doesn't load. Everything else — the build script, the site's own JavaScript, the stylesheet — was written from scratch for this project, with no framework and no bundler.

Typefaces

Playfair Display, Space Mono and Cormorant Garamond, all under the SIL Open Font Licence, served by Google Fonts. The Readable font accessibility setting switches the whole site to Atkinson Hyperlegible, designed by the Braille Institute of America for low-vision readers and also released under the SIL OFL; it is downloaded only if you switch that option on.

Browser platform

The narrator uses your browser's own SpeechSynthesis API; the soundscape uses the Web Audio API; AR and VR use navigator.xr (WebXR). All three are built into the browser — this site ships no speech, audio or tracking service of its own. Bengali and English page translation is handled by the Google Translate website widget.

Hosting

Served as plain static files from GitHub Pages — no server, no database, no build pipeline that must keep running for the site to survive.

Methodological debts

The Endings Project at the University of Victoria, for the digital-longevity principles this build follows. The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance and Mukurtu, for the access-and-consent model. Dublin Core and CIDOC-CRM for metadata vocabulary. The full scholarly bibliography is on Sources & Scholarship.

Corrections

If you believe something on this site is miscredited, misattributed or should not be here at all, please tell us — Contact. Requests from participants and their communities about their own material take precedence over our editorial preferences.

Privacy

What this site knows about you

Nothing, and it has no way to find out. This site uses no analytics, no advertising, no cookies of its own and no tracking of any kind. Your theme, text size, sound setting, narration voice and accessibility options are stored only in your own browser and never leave your device — clearing your browser data erases them completely. Choosing a language opens the page through Google Translate, which necessarily reads the page in order to translate it and is governed by Google's own privacy terms. The field-recording audio is served as plain files from this site, so playing a clip tells us nothing about who played it.

Chitragāthā
