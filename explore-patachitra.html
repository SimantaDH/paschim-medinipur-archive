Patachitra — A Living Tradition of Scroll Art

Painted and sung, scroll by scroll

In Naya village, Pingla Block, every one of 176 households shares the surname Chitrakar — "artist" — and paints. Patachitra is audio-visual storytelling that predates the moving image: a scroll unrolls panel by panel while the artist sings the story live, a performance called pata khelano. That performance, not just the finished painting, is what this archive set out to document.

Natural Pigments, Field-Recorded July 2024

The recipe is the heritage

Every pigment is sourced locally and bound with gum from the bel tree, then sealed in lacquer after curing over a charcoal fire. The recipe matters as much as the finished image.

Patar Gaan — Song of the Painting

Three scrolls, three voices

Every patachitra panel is sung as it is unrolled. These excerpts, recorded in the field in July 2024, show how one painted scroll can hold ecology, devotion and social critique at once.

Shobai Mile Koro Gaach Ropon — on planting trees together+
In Bengali

Shobai mile koro gaach ropon
O Jonogon! Shobai mile koro gaach ropon
Gaach lagaile porey manusher upokaar kore…

In translation

Let us all plant trees together
O People! Let us plant trees together
Planting trees benefits people later…

Performed by Shiuli Chitrakar, Naya, Pingla — a call to ecological responsibility, read alongside the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

Saanjher Belae Jamunar Jol — on Radha and Krishna at dusk+
In Bengali

Saanjher belae jamunar jol ante bole key
Na jaaner kon kalar shone mon mojeche
Kodom tolae uthei abar bongshi bajae ke…

In translation

Who calls for Jamuna water at dusk?
Do you not know who the dark one is who holds my heart?
Who plays the flute again under the kadam tree?…

Performed by Ruksana Chitrakar — dusk as the threshold between the ordinary and the divine, in the Bhakti register of Radha–Krishna devotion.

Dariya Maacher Biye — the wedding of the river fish+
In Bengali

Dariya maacher biye ditey cholo go rangeela
Dui kaatla bole ami biyer palki noye jabolo rangeela…

In translation

O Rangeela, let us go to marry off the river fish
Two katla fish say, we will carry the wedding palanquin…

Performed by Sabera Chitrakar — local biodiversity and Bengali wedding custom, with the boal fish read as a folk allegory for social hierarchy.

Inside a Patua Household

Five roles, one craft collective

Interviews and field documentation across five comparable roles, mirroring the Gajan methodology — plus a literature review spanning twenty-seven articles on the craft.

01

Master Patua / Collective President

Senior artisan holding the collective's institutional memory — pricing, commissions, and representing Naya to outside visitors and press.

02

Senior Woman Patua & Performer

Holds the sung repertoire — the patar gaan that accompanies each scroll — alongside painting practice built over decades.

03

Next-Generation Artisan

Learning the pigment recipes and scroll grammar from family elders while adapting subject matter to new audiences and formats.

04

Market-Facing Patua

Manages sale, exhibition and online presence — the patua most directly navigating the craft's commodification.

05

Community Organiser

Coordinates festival logistics, workshops and the collective's relationship with NGOs and government craft-hub schemes.

What Surprised Us

Patachitra already has a massive digital footprint — almost none of it coordinated

Government heritage portals, individual artist pages, art-market and valuation sites, social and video circulation, press coverage, NGO field reports — six completely separate registers, none talking to each other. That finding is what shaped this archive's own design.
